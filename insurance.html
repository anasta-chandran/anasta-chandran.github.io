Project 01 · Insurance Analytics

Understanding insurance portfolios through data.

An end-to-end team analytics project exploring customer demographics, policy distribution, premiums, coverage and policy status. The objective was to turn a multi-table insurance dataset into useful business insights through structured analysis and dashboards.

01 · The business context

The problem we explored.

Insurance businesses manage information across customers, policies, claims and payments. Looking at these records separately can make it difficult to understand the overall portfolio and identify patterns that may support better decisions.

Our project explored how structured analysis of insurance data could help examine customer profiles, policy distribution, premium amounts, coverage levels and policy status.

Central business question

What can insurance data reveal about customer profiles, policy performance and the distribution of insurance coverage?

02 · Project objectives

What we set out to understand.

Customer profile

Explore customer demographics, age distribution and gender categories.

Policy distribution

Understand the distribution of policies across types and status categories.

Financial overview

Examine premium amounts and coverage values across the policy portfolio.

Claims and payments

Explore claims, settlements and payment information available in the dataset.

03 · Dataset overview

Working with connected business data.

The project involved multiple related datasets covering key areas of insurance operations.

Customer data

Customer demographic information, including age and gender.

Policy data

Policy identifiers, policy types, status, coverage and premium amounts.

Claims data

Claim dates, claim status and settlement information.

Payment data

Payment dates, payment methods and payment status.

04 · Analytical workflow

From raw data to business insights.

01. Data understanding

Reviewed the available tables, fields, data types and categorical values to understand the structure of the insurance dataset.

02. Data preparation

Checked missing values and converted relevant date fields into datetime format for analysis.

03. Data analysis

Used SQL and Python to query, examine and analyse the available insurance information.

04. Dashboard development

Used Excel, Power BI and Tableau to create visual dashboards and communicate analytical results.

06 · Data quality and challenges

Making the data analysis-ready.

An important part of the workflow was checking the quality and consistency of the data before interpreting the results.

Missing values in claims information

Missing values were identified in the claims data, including 1,555 missing settlement dates after date conversion.


Date standardisation

Policy start dates, policy end dates, claim dates, settlement dates and payment dates were converted into datetime format to support time-based analysis.


Multiple related tables

Working across customer, policy, claims and payment information required understanding how different parts of the insurance dataset relate to one another.

07 · Findings and interpretation

What the data revealed.

A varied policy portfolio

Active, terminated and lapsed policies had relatively close counts, suggesting that no single status category overwhelmingly dominated the recorded portfolio.

Multiple-policy ownership

1,300 customers held multiple policies. This provides an opportunity to explore customer relationships and policy ownership patterns further.

Customer age distribution

The 28–38 age group had the highest number of records among the age groups analysed, with 790.

Premium and coverage overview

The average premium amount was 1,052.20 and average coverage amount was 254,562.95 per policy.

08 · Business recommendations

Turning findings into possible actions.

These are proposed opportunities based on the patterns observed, not measured business outcomes.

Review policy retention

Investigate why policies become lapsed or terminated and explore possible retention strategies.

Understand customer segments

Explore customer age groups and policy preferences to support more informed customer segmentation.

Explore cross-selling opportunities

Study customers with multiple policies to identify relevant opportunities for additional coverage.

Improve data quality

Review missing claims settlement information to improve the reliability of claims reporting.
